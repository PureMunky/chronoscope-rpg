Fighting

Enemies flash a red ring before they strike: roll out of the way. Bosses glare a red line before they charge. Out of combat you slowly recover, faster in a village.

Quests change history

The world is a real simulation: peoples grow, farm, fight, catch plague, rise and fall. Elders (!) ask for help with what is actually happening around them. Finishing a quest records a deed in the timeline, and the future is re-simulated from that moment:

  • Raider camps → irrigation (richer soil)
  • Frontier warbands → rallies (solidarity)
  • Moonwort → the plague ends
  • Settlers → empty land is settled
  • Stolen codex → you learn a people's knowledge; teach it to another
  • Granaries → discord in a rival capital
  • Regicide → a rival realm's succession crisis

Gear from other ages

Weapons, armour and ammunition belong to an age, from Stone Age clubs to Industrial rifles. Smiths sell what their people can make, and elite foes drop theirs. Gear from later ages is stronger, but people notice: wield things two or more ages ahead of them (or fire a gun where guns are unknown) and suspicion rises. They whisper, refuse you quests and beds, and at the worst send witch-hunters. Clothes matter too: dress like another people and prices rise; dress two ages off and people walk away from you, refuse to talk, and pay you less (no one attacks you for it). Smiths' markets sell local clothes. Press U to hide everything out of its time: you blend in, but fight with bare fists, without a ranged weapon or armour, until you press U again. Ammunition only comes from an age that can make it. Sell a far-future item to a smith and their people take it apart and learn from it (a deed in history).

Echoes

The Chronoscope lists turning points of your timeline (a plague, a capital sacked, a rival's founding). Take one up with its number and travel to its years to stand in it.

Time

Walking a province takes about a month; resting takes a season. You age only while living, not while travelling in time. When a bearer dies, the Chronoscope passes to someone young, and every deed still stands.
